Date: July 12, 2026

PHILOCHESS 0.2-alpha-001 — Basic Quiescence Search

Implemented the first basic quiescence search in PHILOCHESS.

Technical milestone:
PHILOCHESS can now continue searching tactically unstable positions after the 
normal alpha-beta depth reaches zero.

Instead of immediately applying the static material evaluation at depth zero, 
the engine now examines forcing tactical continuations until it reaches a 
position considered sufficiently quiet for static evaluation.

This is the first version of the PHILOCHESS 0.2 development cycle.
It builds directly on PHILOCHESS 0.1-alpha-032, the validated final version of 
PHILOCHESS 0.1.

Alpha-001 preserves:
- legal move generation;
- FEN loading;
- castling;
- en passant;
- pawn promotion;
- check, checkmate, and stalemate detection;
- perft and divide;
- known reference perft validation;
- material evaluation;
- basic alpha-beta search;
- basic UCI time-control parsing;
- tactical regression testing;
- basic move ordering.

New functionality:
- Added basic quiescence search.
- Alpha-beta now enters quiescence when the normal search depth reaches zero.
- Added stand pat evaluation when the side to move is not in check.
- Added stand pat alpha updates.
- Added stand pat beta cutoffs.
- Added recursive quiescence search using the negamax framework.
- Added captures to the quiescence move set.
- Added promotions to the quiescence move set, including non-capturing 
promotions.
- Deliberately excluded non-capturing checking moves from this first 
quiescence version.
- Added complete legal check-evasion search when the side to move is in check.
- Disabled stand pat when the side to move is in check.
- Added terminal checkmate and stalemate handling inside quiescence.
- Added periodic time-limit checks inside quiescence.
- Added an emergency quiescence safety limit of 64 plies.
- Added a counter for quiescence ply-limit hits.
- Added separate counters for:
  - main-search nodes;
  - quiescence nodes;
  - alpha-beta cutoffs;
  - quiescence beta cutoffs;
  - quiescence nodes entered while in check;
  - selective search depth;
  - quiescence ply-limit hits.
- Updated search information output to report the new quiescence statistics.

Move ordering refinement:
The MVV-LVA-style capture ordering introduced in PHILOCHESS 0.1-alpha-031 was 
reviewed and refined.

The original material values could treat the king as a zero-value attacker 
because the king has a material evaluation value of 0.
Alpha-001 now uses separate piece-ordering ranks for MVV-LVA:

Pawn   = 1
Knight = 2
Bishop = 3
Rook   = 4
Queen  = 5
King   = 6

This keeps material evaluation values separate from move-ordering ranks.

The refined ordering now ensures that:
- the most valuable victim receives priority;
- when two moves capture the same victim, the less valuable attacker receives 
priority;
- the king is no longer treated as the cheapest possible attacker;
- promotions receive their own high-priority category;
- capture-promotions receive both promotion and capture bonuses.

New internal diagnostic commands:
- qprobe
- qcompare [depth]
- qtest

qprobe:
Runs quiescence directly from the current position and reports:
- qscore;
- qnodes;
- quiescence beta cutoffs;
- quiescence check nodes;
- selective depth;
- ply-limit hits.

qcompare [depth]:
Runs the same search twice:
- with MVV-LVA ordering inside quiescence;
- without move ordering inside quiescence.

This allows direct comparison of the resulting move, evaluation, nodes, 
qnodes, and quiescence cutoffs.

qtest:
Runs four controlled internal tests for:
- horizon recapture avoidance;
- capture-sequence resolution;
- non-capturing promotion inclusion;
- check evasion without stand pat.

Current limitations:
- The static evaluation remains material-only.
- Quiescence currently searches captures and promotions only when the side to 
move is not in check.
- Non-capturing checking moves are deliberately excluded.
- No static exchange evaluation is implemented.
- No SEE pruning is implemented.
- No delta pruning is implemented.
- No transposition table is implemented.
- No hash move ordering is implemented.
- No principal variation move ordering is implemented.
- No killer move heuristic is implemented.
- No history heuristic is implemented.
- No iterative deepening is implemented.
- Maximum normal search depth remains 4.
- The principal variation output still reports only the selected root move.
- Time management remains basic.
- stop is recognized, but search is still synchronous and cannot yet be 
interrupted asynchronously.
- go infinite is parsed, but true infinite analysis is not implemented.
- Quiescence nodes can significantly outnumber normal search nodes in tactical 
positions.
- The 64-ply limit is an emergency safety mechanism, not a replacement for 
future selective pruning.
- The tactical regression suite is still small and should be expanded with 
additional controlled positions and future errors found in PHILOCHESS games.
- The engine does not yet evaluate piece placement, mobility, king safety, 
pawn structure, development, activity, coordination, or initiative.
- Draw adjudication by the fifty-move rule, repetition, or insufficient 
material is not implemented.
- The current Debug build is not optimized for speed.
- External Myracle tournament validation of alpha-001 remains pending.

New internal diagnostic commands:
- inventorytest [depth]
- fullregression [depth]

inventorytest [depth]:
Executes the complete 64-case inventory. If no depth is supplied, category [B]
search targets use diagnostic depth 4.

The command reports for every case:
- its stable identifier;
- category;
- result state;
- test title;
- the relevant legal, tactical, search, node, or evaluation data.

It concludes with:
- PASS, FAIL, OPEN, BASELINE, and TOTAL counts;
- category counts;
- an inventory structure result.

fullregression [depth]:
Provides an alias for the same complete inventory execution.

Meaning of result states:
- PASS means that the case fulfilled its implemented criterion.
- FAIL means that the test infrastructure or required criterion failed.
- OPEN means that the position and search executed correctly, but the 
documented
  tactical target was not demonstrated at the tested depth.
- BASELINE means that a category [C] position was loaded and verified as a
  reference without requiring positional understanding that the engine does not
  yet possess.

PHILOCHESS 0.2-alpha-003 — Iterative Deepening

Implemented the first iterative-deepening search in PHILOCHESS.

Technical milestone:
PHILOCHESS can now search successively from depth 1 to the requested depth,
preserve the result of the last fully completed iteration, and use the previous
iteration's best root move to guide the next iteration.

This is the third version of the PHILOCHESS 0.2 development cycle.
It builds directly on the validated PHILOCHESS 0.2-alpha-002 code without
modifying that closed version.

Alpha-003 preserves:
- legal move generation and move application;
- FEN loading;
- castling;
- en passant;
- pawn promotion;
- check, checkmate, and stalemate detection;
- perft, divide, and known reference perft validation;
- material evaluation;
- alpha-beta search;
- quiescence search;
- refined MVV-LVA move ordering;
- UCI mate-score output;
- basic UCI time-control parsing;
- the four-case quiescence correctness suite;
- the inherited three-position tactical regression test;
- the complete 64-case tactical regression and quiescence inventory;
- all diagnostic commands inherited from alpha-002.

New functionality:
- Replaced the normal single fixed-depth playing search with iterative
  deepening.
- Added successive completed searches at depths 1, 2, 3, and 4 when depth 4 is
  requested.
- Added requested-depth and completed-iteration tracking to the search result.
- Preserved the best move and score only after an iteration has been completed
  in full.
- Added a legal fallback root move before the first iteration begins.
- If a time limit interrupts an iteration, the engine now returns the best move
  from the last fully completed iteration instead of committing a partial
  result.
- The best move from the previous completed iteration is searched first at the
  root of the next iteration.
- Search statistics accumulate across all iterations executed by one go
  command.
- All iterations share the same search start time and time limit.
- Added final search reporting of the number of completed iterations compared
  with the requested number.
- Preserved a dedicated fixed-depth path for the diagnostic regression
  inventory, maintaining controlled comparison with alpha-002.
- Updated UCI identification to:
  id name PHILOCHESS 0.2-alpha-003

New internal diagnostic commands:
- idtest [depth]
- iterativetest [depth]

Both command names execute the same iterative-deepening test suite.

The suite runs controlled searches from the initial position and verifies for
each requested depth that:
- the engine has a legal best move;
- the search is not stopped;
- the requested depth is recorded correctly;
- every iteration from depth 1 through the requested depth is completed;
- the final completed depth equals the requested depth.

Version boundary:
Alpha-003 reports one final UCI search-information block and an iteration
summary. It does not yet emit a complete principal variation or a separate UCI
information line after every completed iteration.

Complete principal variation tracking and the fuller presentation of iterative
UCI reports belong mainly to alpha-004.

Important note:
Alpha-003 does not change the static evaluation function.
PHILOCHESS still uses material-only evaluation.

Iterative deepening reorganises the search and provides a stable completed
result under time pressure. By itself, it does not add positional knowledge or
guarantee a major immediate increase in playing strength.

PHILOCHESS 0.2-alpha-006 — Time-Controlled Iterative Deepening

Implemented the first effective time-controlled iterative-deepening system in
PHILOCHESS.

Technical milestone:
PHILOCHESS can now use an available UCI time budget to continue iterative
deepening beyond the former normal depth ceiling of 4.

The engine calculates separate soft and hard time limits, continues searching
while completed iterations remain within the soft limit, and interrupts the
active iteration when the hard limit is reached.

Only fully completed iterations are committed. If an iteration is interrupted,
its provisional move, score, and principal variation are discarded, and the
engine returns the result of the last completed iteration.

This is the sixth version of the PHILOCHESS 0.2 development cycle.
It builds directly on the validated PHILOCHESS 0.2-alpha-005 code without
modifying that closed version.

Alpha-006 preserves:
- legal move generation and move application;
- FEN loading and all special chess moves;
- check, checkmate, and stalemate detection;
- perft and known reference perft validation;
- material-only evaluation;
- alpha-beta and quiescence search;
- iterative deepening;
- complete principal-variation construction;
- iterative UCI information reporting;
- UCI mate-score reporting;
- retention of the last fully completed iteration;
- PV-move priority at the root and below the root;
- promotion priority and MVV-LVA capture ordering;
- the established tactical and quiescence regression suites;
- the complete 64-case diagnostic inventory;
- all diagnostic commands inherited from alpha-005.

New functionality:
- Added separate soft and hard time limits.
- Added time-controlled iterative deepening beyond depth 4.
- Added an absolute search-depth safety ceiling of 64.
- Added side-to-move selection of wtime or btime.
- Added side-to-move selection of winc or binc.
- Added functional handling of movestogo.
- Added a default estimate of 55 moves to go when movestogo is absent.
- Added a dedicated allocation for explicit movetime commands.
- Added a small emergency clock reserve.
- Added a legal emergency move before the first iteration begins.
- Added hard-limit checks inside the normal and quiescence searches.
- Added soft-limit checks between fully completed iterations.
- Added safe rejection of an interrupted iteration.
- Preserved the best move, evaluation, and PV from the last completed
  iteration.
- Preserved explicit go depth N as an authoritative depth request.
- Added diagnostic reporting of the soft limit, hard limit, elapsed time,
  interrupted depth, and number of completed iterations.
- Updated UCI identification to:
  id name PHILOCHESS 0.2-alpha-006

New internal diagnostic command:
- timetest

The command verifies:
- the time allocation for a 15+10 control;
- an explicit search at depth 5;
- the availability of a legal result with a one-millisecond budget;
- that a larger movetime produces a non-decreasing completed depth and can
  exceed depth 4.

Time-allocation formula:

When movetime is specified:

hard limit = movetime

soft limit = 85% of the hard limit

When the side-to-move clock is supplied:

target time =
remaining time / moves to go + increment / 2

The target time becomes the hard limit unless it would consume the emergency
clock reserve.

The reserve is limited to a range from 50 to 1000 milliseconds. Under normal
15+10 conditions, this reserve does not alter the calculated target.

The soft limit is 85% of the final hard limit.

With 900,000 ms remaining, a 10,000 ms increment, and the default estimate of
55 moves to go, PHILOCHESS calculates:

hard limit:
900000 / 55 + 10000 / 2 = 21363 ms

soft limit:
21363 × 85% = 18158 ms

The soft limit is consulted only between iterations. A completed iteration may
therefore finish slightly after that limit. The hard limit is checked during
the search and stops an active iteration.

Version boundary:
Alpha-006 changes time allocation, search interruption, and the depth available
to time-controlled or explicitly deeper UCI searches.

It does not modify:
- the static evaluation function;
- the legal-move generator;
- the quiescence rules;
- the established move-ordering hierarchy;
- principal-variation construction;
- the tactical objectives of the regression inventory;
- positional or endgame knowledge.

Important note:
The default validation depth remains 4, but time-managed searches without an
explicit depth may now deepen progressively up to the absolute safety ceiling
of 64.

An explicit go depth N command may also request a depth greater than 4.

Alpha-006 implements a functional and safe first time-management layer. The
formula is intentionally static. It does not yet adapt the budget according to
position complexity, best-move stability, evaluation changes, branching
factor, game phase, or the estimated cost of the next iteration.

PHILOCHESS 0.2-alpha-007 — Piece-Square Tables

Implemented the first positional evaluation layer in PHILOCHESS through
conservative piece-square tables.

Technical milestone:
PHILOCHESS no longer evaluates positions exclusively by material.

The static evaluation now combines the established material balance with
piece-square values that reward or penalise each piece according to its
location on the board.

This is the seventh version of the PHILOCHESS 0.2 development cycle.

Supporting design:
The implemented values follow the approved PHILOCHESS PST v1 design: an
original, deliberately conservative first proposal expressed in centipawns.

The design contains six types of piece-square tables and seven numerical
matrices:
- pawn;
- knight;
- bishop;
- rook;
- queen;
- king in the middlegame;
- king in the endgame.

Board orientation:
PHILOCHESS maps:
- a1 to board[0];
- h1 to board[7];
- a8 to board[56];
- h8 to board[63].

The seven matrices are therefore stored rank 1 first and rank 8 last.

White pieces use the table square directly.

Black pieces use a vertical reflection:

oriented square = square ^ 56

This preserves the files and gives both colours the same relative values
without exchanging the king and queen flanks.

Game-phase calculation:
The global phase uses the following piece weights:
- knight: 1;
- bishop: 1;
- rook: 2;
- queen: 4;
- pawn: 0;
- king: 0.

The complete initial material produces phase 24.
Kings alone produce phase 0.

Only the king uses two phase-dependent tables in PST v1.

The interpolated king value is:

king PST =
(middlegame value × phase
+ endgame value × (24 - phase)) / 24

The other five piece types use a single conservative table throughout the
game.

Static-evaluation composition:

static evaluation =
material evaluation + piece-square evaluation

The material values remain unchanged:
- pawn: 100;
- knight: 320;
- bishop: 330;
- rook: 500;
- queen: 900;
- king: 0.

The complete White-perspective evaluation is calculated first. Its sign is
then adjusted only once according to the side to move, preserving the
convention required by negamax.

Version boundary:
Alpha-007 changes the static evaluation and, consequently, may change the
selected move, numerical score, principal variation, node count, and
diagnostic status of exact-move regression cases.

It does not modify:
- the legal-move generator;
- move application;
- the material values;
- alpha-beta search rules;
- quiescence move-selection rules;
- the move-ordering hierarchy;
- principal-variation construction;
- the time-allocation formula;
- search interruption;
- the objectives or structure of the 64-case inventory.

The PST values are intentionally replaceable. Future versions may tune or
replace them without changing the legal-move generator or the basic search
architecture.

Implemented the first explicit piece-activity evaluation in PHILOCHESS through

Supporting design:
The implemented rules follow the approved PHILOCHESS Mobility v1 design: an
original, deliberately conservative first approximation to piece activity,
expressed in centipawns.

Mobility v1 counts occupancy-based pseudo-legal destinations for:
- knights;
- bishops;
- rooks;
- queens.

Pawns and kings are not included.

The approved weights are:
- knight destination: 4 centipawns;
- bishop destination: 3 centipawns;
- rook destination: 2 centipawns;
- queen destination: 1 centipawn.

The final term is bilateral:

Mobility v1 =
weighted White mobility - weighted Black mobility

Counting rules:
- a destination occupied by a friendly piece does not count;
- a destination occupied by an enemy piece counts;
- a sliding piece stops at the first occupied square;
- a knight may jump over occupied squares;
- destinations attacked by the opponent still count;
- absolute pins do not reduce the count;
- the calculation does not test whether moving a piece would expose its own
  king to check.

These rules keep Mobility v1 deterministic and independent of the legal-move
generator. They also make its exact output suitable for a dedicated regression
inventory.

Static-evaluation composition:

static evaluation =
material evaluation + piece-square evaluation + mobility evaluation

The material values remain unchanged:
- pawn: 100;
- knight: 320;
- bishop: 330;
- rook: 500;
- queen: 900;
- king: 0.

The PST values, game-phase calculation, and king-table interpolation remain
unchanged from PHILOCHESS PST v1.

The complete White-perspective evaluation is calculated first. Its sign is
then adjusted only once according to the side to move, preserving the
convention required by negamax.

Supporting test inventory:
Before coding, a complete 64-position Mobility v1 inventory was prepared,
reviewed, and verified:
- 44 controlled positions;
- 10 transversal positions;
- 10 integrated positions.

The controlled positions isolate the counting rules for individual pieces,
friendly and enemy blockers, captures, board edges, and colour symmetry.

The transversal positions verify that the same principles remain valid across
different piece types and board arrangements.

The integrated positions combine several pieces and occupancy effects in the
same position and verify the final bilateral Mobility v1 score.

PHILOCHESS played an energetic and active game. It placed the Black king under

ended.

The draw was not caused by an illegal move, a tactical collapse, a time loss, a
crash, or a communication failure. It exposed a missing terminal rule: a
winning engine must reject a drawing repetition when a superior continuation
is available.

Game 2 — Zoidberg 0.1 vs PHILOCHESS 0.2-alpha-008

Result: 1-0

The exchange of queens led to an ending in which Zoidberg gradually obtained
the more comfortable position. PHILOCHESS entered a knight ending and attempted
to contain a dangerous passed h-pawn by transferring its knight to the kingside.

The defensive plan addressed the immediate danger, but left the queenside
insufficiently protected. The White king penetrated and captured Black's
queenside pawns. Zoidberg then gave up its knight to simplify the position and 
was left with decisive passed pawns. One of them promoted, and the new queen, 
supported by the king, produced a forced mate. PHILOCHESS announced the impending mate, 
after which Myracle adjudicated the game in White’s favor before checkmate was delivered 
on the board.

PHILOCHESS continued playing legally and recognised the approaching forced mate
before the final checkmate. The game highlighted the present absence of
dedicated passed-pawn evaluation and the difficulty of coordinating king and
knight against threats on both wings.

Operational rule:

Stalemate occurs when the player to move:
- has no legal move; and
- is not in check.

The terminal distinction implemented in the search is:

Zero legal moves + king in check     = checkmate
Zero legal moves + king not in check = stalemate

Stalemate ends the game immediately and is evaluated as a draw:

stalemate score = 0

The terminal state is examined before stand pat or the ordinary static
evaluation. Therefore, material, PST, and mobility advantages cannot overwrite
the score of a terminal stalemate.

Search integration:

At the root, a terminal position returns:
- no legal best move;
- score 0 for stalemate;
- bestmove 0000 in UCI output;
- one recorded stalemate node;
- zero subsequent static evaluations.

Inside alpha-beta and quiescence, the terminal value is returned immediately
and propagated through negamax with the correct sign convention.

This permits four distinct search behaviours:
- choosing mate instead of immediate stalemate;
- retaining a winning continuation instead of surrendering the advantage;
- finding a forced stalemate as a defensive resource;
- rejecting a false stalemate resource when the superior side can avoid it.

queen promotion, which stalemates, and bishop or knight promotion, which leads

This support exists only to preserve the exact oracle of the two stalemate
tests. It is not the complete dead-position implementation and does not replace

Time-test harness correction:

The initial Release execution of timetest returned 4/4. The first Debug
execution returned 3/4 even though the engine behaved correctly.

The depths observed in Debug were:

* 50 ms:   depth 2;
* 2000 ms: depth 3.

The fourth test was intended to verify that additional thinking time did not
produce a shallower completed search. This requirement was satisfied. However,
the test also contained the following internal condition:

longResult.depth > SEARCH_MAX_DEPTH

This required the 2000 ms search to exceed depth 4. The threshold depended on
compiler optimisation and machine performance rather than on the functional
behaviour being tested. It was satisfied in Release, which reached depth 5,
but not in Debug, which reached depth 3 while still respecting the time limit
and searching more deeply than the 50 ms run.

The performance-dependent threshold was therefore removed. The corrected test
retains the relevant requirements:

* both searches must return a legal move;
* both searches must operate under time management;
* the longer search must not complete at a shallower depth;
* elapsed time must remain within the established margins;
* the returned principal variation must be legal.

No search or time-management logic was changed. The correction affected only
the test harness.

configurations.

Operational rule:

- zero legal moves and king not in check: stalemate;

  position;

A dead position is evaluated as a draw:

dead-position score = 0

When a dead root still has legal moves, PHILOCHESS retains a legal move as its
bestmove, even though the position is drawn. bestmove 0000 remains reserved
for roots with no legal moves, such as checkmate or stalemate.

unnecessary ordinary search.

propagated through negamax using the correct sign convention.

Transitions into dead positions can therefore:

- secure a draw for the inferior side;
- prevent a material advantage from being treated as a win;
- avoid unnecessary search after mate impossibility has been proved;
- coexist without interfering with checkmate and stalemate.

Historical-state and counter rule:

PHILOCHESS uses the fifth field of a complete FEN as the halfmove clock:

halfmoveClock

The sixth field remains the independent fullmove number:

fullmoveNumber

The fifty-move rule uses only halfmoveClock.

After every legal move, the counter follows this rule:

- a pawn move resets halfmoveClock to 0;
- a capture resets halfmoveClock to 0;
- every other legal move increments halfmoveClock by exactly 1.

The reset applies to:

- ordinary pawn moves;
- double pawn advances;
- ordinary captures;
- en-passant captures;
- noncapturing promotions;
- capturing promotions.

The increment applies to:

- quiet king moves;
- quiet knight moves;
- quiet bishop moves;
- quiet rook moves;
- quiet queen moves;
- castling;
- quiet checking moves.

Giving check does not reset the counter. Castling does not reset it either.
The fullmove number continues to advance independently after Black moves.

Operational search rule:

Classification follows this order:

- zero legal moves and king in check: checkmate;
- zero legal moves and king not in check: stalemate;
- one or more legal moves and bilateral mate impossibility proved: dead
  position;
- otherwise, halfmove clock greater than or equal to 100:
  FiftyMoveDraw;
- otherwise: nonterminal position.

The corresponding scores are:

- checkmate: mate score adjusted by search ply;
- stalemate: 0;
- dead position: 0;
- FiftyMoveDraw: 0.

Checkmate therefore retains absolute precedence at the threshold. Stalemate
and dead position also retain their established classifications.

Check alone is not terminal. If the king is in check but has legal evasions
and halfmoveClock is at least 100, the search result is FiftyMoveDraw.

Root and UCI behaviour:

A root position with halfmoveClock at least 100 and legal moves is assigned
score 0 without entering unnecessary ordinary search or static evaluation.

PHILOCHESS still preserves and returns a legal bestmove. It does not return
bestmove 0000 merely because the halfmove clock has reached 100.

Reaching the threshold does not terminate the engine process. It terminates
the evaluation of that search node with score 0. In actual play, result
adjudication remains the responsibility of the configured GUI.

This separation allows PHILOCHESS to consider the rule during calculation
while remaining able to provide a valid UCI move whenever legal moves exist.

Search integration:

At a nonterminal root below 100, all legal candidate moves remain available
for comparison.

A quiet move from halfmove clock 99 creates a child with value 100. That
child is recognised as a draw and returns score 0.

A pawn move or capture from 99 resets the child counter to 0. Search can
therefore continue evaluating that branch normally.

As a result, PHILOCHESS can:

- reject a drawing branch when a winning reset is available;
- continue examining root siblings after encountering a drawing branch;
- choose a drawing branch when every alternative is worse;
- propagate score 0 through one or more negamax levels;
- preserve checkmate over branches that reach the threshold.

Inside quiescence, FiftyMoveDraw is recognised before:

- stand pat;
- static evaluation;
- capture expansion;
- ordinary check-evasion expansion.

A capture that resets the counter prevents a false positive and allows
quiescence to continue normally.

PHILOCHESS 0.2-alpha-012 — Threefold Repetition Detection in Search

Date: August 21, 2026

PHILOCHESS 0.2-alpha-012 — Threefold Repetition Detection in Search

Threefold-repetition detection was implemented and internally validated as a
search result.

Technical milestone:

PHILOCHESS can now reconstruct and preserve the sequence of chess positions
that led to the current root, combine that game history with the active search
path, and recognize the third or any later occurrence of the same position.

An eligible repeated position receives draw value and score 0 at the root,
inside the main alpha-beta search, and inside quiescence search. The result can
therefore affect move selection: PHILOCHESS can reject repetition while ahead
when a better continuation exists, or choose repetition when every alternative
is worse.

This is the twelfth version of the PHILOCHESS 0.2 development cycle. It is
based directly on the validated PHILOCHESS 0.2-alpha-011 source without

Position identity:

Two history entries represent the same position only when all of the following
are equal:

- every piece on every square;
- the side to move;
- all current castling rights;
- the effective en-passant possibility.

The FEN halfmove and fullmove fields do not affect this comparison. They remain
available for their own rules and diagnostics, but they cannot prevent two
otherwise identical positions from counting as repetition occurrences.

The effective-en-passant rule prevents both kinds of error tested by the
inventory. A legal en-passant capture distinguishes the positions. A nominal
target with no possible legal capture, including a pseudo-legal capture made
illegal by a pin, does not distinguish them.

History and search-path model:

The UCI position command reconstructs the complete sequence beginning with P0,
rather than storing only the final FEN. The current position is therefore
already present exactly once when search begins.

During search, each legal child contributes its identity to the active path.
The occurrence count for a node is the sum of matching entries in the relevant
UCI history and the active path, without double-counting the root.

Every pushed search identity is removed on return, including beta cutoffs and
repetition returns. Consequently, exploration order cannot leak a position
from one sibling into another.

Irreversible moves delimit the relevant segment. Ordinary captures,
en-passant captures, pawn moves and promotions prevent positions before that
boundary from being counted as later occurrences of the new segment.

Search behavior:

The repetition check is active at the root, in alpha-beta, and in quiescence.
At the third occurrence or later, the node returns score 0 before a material,
PST, mobility, auxiliary evaluation, or stand-pat score can replace the
rule-determined result.

The draw score is then compared normally with the scores of sibling moves.
This produces the intended strategic behavior:

- a winning side rejects repetition when a continuation scores above 0;
- a losing side can seek repetition when the alternatives score below 0;
- repetition can prevail over an adverse mating line;
- a mating win still prevails over an available repetition;

Root and UCI boundary:

or adjudicate the external game result. The GUI remains responsible for the
official game termination.

a legal move for bestmove. This preserves UCI continuity. A terminal stalemate
still returns bestmove 0000 because no legal move exists.

Game 1 — PHILOCHESS 0.2-alpha-012 vs. Zoidberg 0.1

Result: 0-1

PHILOCHESS played White and obtained a large opening and middlegame
advantage. It exploited Black's lack of coordination, penetrated the seventh
rank with a rook, applied pressure on both wings, and kept the exposed black
king under pressure. At its peak, the white position received an evaluation
close to +10.

The engine did not find a conclusive conversion. It preferred material gains
and lateral expansion, allowed Zoidberg to reorganize, and repeatedly let the
advantage decline before new opposing errors enlarged it again.

The decisive turning point was 50.Rf8+??. The resulting liquidation removed
the active rook and the passed e-pawn, extinguished the initiative, and led to
an approximately equal position. After the queens were exchanged,
PHILOCHESS entered an ending one pawn down but with reasonable resistance.

An imprecise king maneuver allowed Black's passed h-pawn to advance. Black
then created a second promotion threat with the g-pawn. PHILOCHESS sought
counterplay with its own passer instead of stopping the more urgent threat,
allowing Black to promote and making mate inevitable.

Game 2 — Zoidberg 0.1 vs. PHILOCHESS 0.2-alpha-012

Result: 1-0 by adjudication

PHILOCHESS played Black and was inferior from the opening. Premature queen
moves, castling before the pieces were coordinated, and repeated omission of
a useful central break gave White the initiative.

Zoidberg installed a knight on f7, attacking both black rooks. The principal
turning point was 10...Rxd1+?, which accepted an unfavorable liquidation.
Although Black's other knight later captured the white rook on a1,
PHILOCHESS lost both rooks and entered a materially inferior position.
Zoidberg retained a rook while its knight on h8 remained temporarily isolated
and out of play.

PHILOCHESS reorganized its minor pieces and generated threats against the
white king. Two Zoidberg inaccuracies briefly reduced the advantage and gave
Black reasonable resistance chances. PHILOCHESS then favored centralization
and apparent bishop activity instead of the required defensive blockade.
White obtained a dangerous passed f-pawn.

Checks and tactical counterplay did not stop the pawn. Once it reached the
seventh rank and its principal blockers were removed, promotion became
inevitable. Myracle adjudicated the decisive position in White's favor.
