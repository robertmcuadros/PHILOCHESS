PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-001 — UCI Infinite Analysis and Search Control

Implemented true UCI Infinite analysis and concurrent search control in
PHILOCHESS.

Technical milestone:

PHILOCHESS can now keep an Infinite search active until an external stop or
quit command is received, while the UCI command loop remains responsive.

The engine can process stop, isready, and quit while search work is active,
preserve the last fully completed iteration, discard incomplete work safely,
and close the search worker without leaving residual output or active work.

This is the first version of the PHILOCHESS 0.3 development cycle.

It builds directly on the validated and closed
PHILOCHESS 0.2-alpha-013 — Draw-Rule Integration and Cross-Rule Regression
without modifying that closed version.

Baseline problem:

PHILOCHESS 0.2-alpha-013 recognised the UCI token:

go infinite

but did not yet implement its full semantics.

The inherited SearchLimits state retained:

SEARCH_DEFAULT_DEPTH = 1

as the effective depth ceiling when Infinite was requested without another
authoritative finite limit.

The observable baseline behaviour was therefore:

info string go limits depth 1 infinite
info depth 1 ...
bestmove ...

The search ended automatically after depth 1. Because go was processed
synchronously, the UCI loop could not process stop, isready, or quit while the
search was still active.

The failures were intentional baseline evidence, not unexpected defects in the

Effective search modes:

Infinite
    No automatic depth or time termination.
    Ends through stop or quit, except for roots that are already terminal under
    the engine's established chess-rule contracts.

FixedDepth
    Ends after completing the explicitly requested depth.

MoveTime
    Ends according to the requested fixed time budget.

ClockTime
    Ends according to the inherited budget calculated from the clock and
    increment of the side to move.

Infinite precedence:

Commands such as:

go wtime 1000 btime 1000 winc 0 binc 0 movestogo 10 infinite

and:

go depth 2 movetime 100 infinite

are treated as Infinite searches.

The finite values may remain available for diagnostic reporting, but they do
not become automatic termination causes for that invocation.

Search-control contract:

A completed iteration is the atomic public search result.

If depth k is the last fully completed iteration and stop interrupts depth
k + 1:

- depth k + 1 is discarded as incomplete;
- the score from depth k is preserved;
- the principal variation from depth k is preserved;
- bestmove is derived from the last committed result;
- no partial depth-k+1 information is published as a completed iteration.

The final implementation reports active interruption through diagnostics such
as:

info string external stop request observed
info string discarded incomplete iteration at depth N

These diagnostics make the interruption path observable without changing the
UCI bestmove contract.

UCI concurrency:

The UCI loop remains responsive during active search.

An isready command sent while Infinite is running receives:

readyok

without terminating or restarting the active search.

The same go continues deepening after readyok.

Stop terminates the active search and produces exactly one bestmove.

Quit can terminate the process while Infinite work is still active; it is not
necessary to send stop first.

Practical Match Validation — Myracle Chess GUI

After formal alpha-001 acceptance and the historical Release regression, the
accepted Release executable was subjected to four practical match games in
Myracle Chess GUI v1.0 build 118.

These games were practical diagnostic tests rather than acceptance-inventory
cases. Their purpose was to observe PHILOCHESS under normal competitive
conditions and identify chess-playing weaknesses that are outside the direct
scope of UCI Infinite Analysis and Search Control.

Opponents:

- Zoidberg 0.1;
- Shallow Blue 2.0.0.

Conditions:

- time control: 900+10, equivalent to 15 minutes plus 10 seconds per move;
- normal initial position;
- Release | x64 executable;
- no opening book;
- no Syzygy tablebases;
- ponder disabled;
- full balanced adjudication without tablebases;
- one game with each color against each opponent.

Results:

- PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-001 vs. Zoidberg 0.1: 0-1;
- Zoidberg 0.1 vs. PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-001: 1-0;
- PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-001 vs. Shallow Blue 2.0.0: 0-1;
- Shallow Blue 2.0.0 vs. PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-001: 1-0.

Aggregate result:

- vs. Zoidberg 0.1: 0/2;
- vs. Shallow Blue 2.0.0: 0/2;
- total: 0/4.

Game observations:

1. PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-001 vs. Zoidberg 0.1

PHILOCHESS emerged from the opening with a clearly favorable position
based on better central control, more kingside space, and a lead in
development. It did not convert those advantages into coordinated
action. Its pieces were reasonably placed but did not work as a unit,
while Zoidberg reorganized and reduced the pressure. PHILOCHESS also
underestimated both the size of its initial advantage and the
seriousness of the later deterioration, especially in the pawn
structure. The defeat was therefore less the result of one isolated
error than of difficulty coordinating the pieces, recognizing critical
moments, and converting strategic superiority into concrete play.

2. Zoidberg 0.1 vs. PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-001

From the opening, PHILOCHESS came under pressure because its development
did not harmonize with the demands of the center, allowing Zoidberg to
establish an early advantage. The black position lost cohesion before
development was completed. Although PHILOCHESS later penetrated with its
rooks, the activity was not sufficiently supported by the rest of the
army, while Zoidberg consolidated its material advantage. PHILOCHESS
also underestimated the seriousness of its position for many moves.
Once rook activity could no longer compensate for structural weaknesses
and poor coordination, the ending became clearly favorable for White.
The game exposed a recurring practical problem: PHILOCHESS can develop
and activate pieces without always integrating them into a common plan.

3. PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-001 vs. Shallow Blue 2.0.0

Thanks to its central presence, PHILOCHESS obtained a promising position
but weakened the kingside too early. The move 14.gxh5 allowed the
opposing knight to join the attack decisively, whereas 14.g5, already
played by an earlier PHILOCHESS version against the same opponent, kept
the sector closed. The white king then became exposed and several pieces
were forced into defensive duties. Shallow Blue gave back almost all of
its advantage with 20...c5, allowing the knight to reach the strong d6
square and giving PHILOCHESS good chances to reorganize. However,
PHILOCHESS did not fully recognize that king safety remained the central
problem. With 25.Nxe4 it restored material equality at the cost of
allowing the bishop to join the attack. A final defensive opportunity
was later missed. The game again showed that PHILOCHESS can find useful
local resources without always evaluating the opponent’s coordination or 
king-safety urgency correctly.

4. Shallow Blue 2.0.0 vs. PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-001

Faced with a demanding opening, PHILOCHESS remained competitive despite
some imprecisions, relying on activity in the center and on the
kingside. On several occasions it could have sacrificed the exchange on
f3 to open lines and obtain attacking chances, but instead preferred to
retain material and seek activity with isolated pieces. After difficult
middlegame phases, PHILOCHESS reached an objectively equal rook ending
and held it for several moves. At that point the central issue was the
coordination of rook, king, and pawns. The serious error 59...Rb1+ lost a
valuable tempo that could have been used to bring the king toward the
queenside and coordinate it with the rook against the opposing passed
pawn. The later simplification led to a lost pawn ending and exposed
insufficient understanding of when tactical activity must give way to
defensive coordination.

These practical results do not alter the 64/64 technical acceptance of
alpha-001. Alpha-001 was designed to implement and validate UCI Infinite
Analysis and Search Control, not to redesign chess evaluation or produce a
direct playing-strength increase.

The four games are retained as practical diagnostic evidence for later search
and evaluation work in the PHILOCHESS 0.3 development cycle.

Current limitations:

- Alpha-001 changes UCI search control and concurrency; it does not directly
  redesign the chess evaluation.
- The inherited static evaluation remains based mainly on material,
  PHILOCHESS PST v1, and PHILOCHESS Mobility v1.
- King-safety evaluation remains limited.
- Pawn-structure evaluation remains limited.
- Passed-pawn, promotion-distance, and blockade evaluation remain limited.
- Piece activity, coordination, and initiative remain only partially
  represented.
- Endgame-specific knowledge remains limited.
- Practical match testing reinforced the need for stronger piece coordination,
  king-safety awareness, evaluation of positional deterioration, and
  king-rook-pawn coordination in endings.
- No Zobrist hashing is implemented.
- No transposition table is implemented.
- No hash-move ordering is implemented.
- No killer-move heuristic is implemented.
- No history heuristic is implemented.
- No aspiration windows are implemented.
- No principal-variation search is implemented.
- No static exchange evaluation or SEE-based pruning is implemented.
- No late-move reductions are implemented.
- Quiescence still requires later optimisation and selective-pruning work.
- Time management remains the inherited conservative first implementation.
- The engine source remains monolithic and is scheduled for modularisation in
  the next alpha.

PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-002 — Engine Modularization and Interface Separation

The engine inherited from PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-001 was reorganized, moving
from a monolithic source file to a modular structure with explicit
responsibilities, dependency rules, and interfaces.

Technical milestone:

The chess engine, UCI protocol, search controller, diagnostics, tests, board
representation, evaluation, rules, and search algorithms no longer belong to
the same monolithic implementation unit.

Each area now has a defined module and communicates with the others through
explicit headers and interfaces.

The architectural principle governing this version is:

“Alpha-002 is an architectural transformation, not a search-strength
transformation.”

PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-002 therefore intentionally preserves the chess
behaviour, search decisions, evaluation values, time-control semantics, draw
rules, and UCI search-control contracts established and validated in
alpha-001.

No intentional playing-strength improvement was introduced.

This is the second version of the PHILOCHESS 0.3 development cycle.

It builds directly on the validated and closed version:

PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-001 — UCI Infinite Analysis and Search Control

without modifying that closed baseline.

Baseline:

The exact alpha-001 source used for the transformation has the following
SHA-256 fingerprint:

d30c8f7834bcbb777e6c898397fb434235acb85effb52f85d356b4c165ac1091

That baseline already contained:

* true Infinite analysis;
* asynchronous UCI search control;
* FixedDepth, MoveTime, ClockTime, and Infinite modes;
* concurrent stop, isready, and quit processing;
* complete principal-variation reporting;
* MultiPV;
* integrated draw-rule handling;
* the IASC-A06 FEN FIX for compatibility with ChessBase Reader 2017;
* the deterministic quiescence barrier used by the IASC-A02 tests.

Alpha-002 preserved the functional FEN compatibility fix and isolated the
IASC-A02 hooks as Debug-only test infrastructure.

Scope of the transformation:

* The monolithic engine was split into coherent implementation modules.
* Explicit public headers were introduced for the main engine domains.
* Permitted dependencies between modules were defined.
* Direct UCI output was removed from the chess and search layers.
* Normal UCI reporting was separated from internal diagnostic reporting.
* Technical diagnostics were preserved for testing and development.
* A UCI option was added to enable or disable technical diagnostics.
* The asynchronous search worker introduced in alpha-001 was preserved.
* All established search modes and time-control semantics were preserved.
* The complete historical regression infrastructure and development commands
  were preserved.
* The Visual Studio project and filters were updated for the modular
  structure.
* Equivalent relevant behaviour was preserved between Release and Debug.
* Deterministic test hooks were excluded from the production Release
  executable.

Dependency rule:

The chess-domain layers do not know about UCI, diagnostics, or tests.

The diagnostics module does not emit UCI output directly. It produces
diagnostic data or formatted lines, and the UCI layer decides whether those
lines may be sent.

The resulting dependency direction is:

* the core module depends only on the standard library;
* the chess module depends on core;
* the evaluation module depends on core and chess;
* the rules module depends on core and chess;
* the search module depends on core, chess, evaluation, and rules;
* the diagnostics module may inspect the chess-domain interfaces;
* UCI owns protocol interpretation and depends on the required engine
  modules;
* the tests module uses the expressly provided production and internal test
  interfaces.

Main board and rule interfaces:

* Position.h exposes position and FEN operations without UCI dependencies.
* MoveGeneration.h owns attacks, pseudo-legal moves, legal moves, and
  makeMove.
* MoveApplication.h applies validated UCI move text without writing to the
  console.
* Repetition.h contains repetition keys and reversible-history operations.
* DrawRules.h exposes the production draw-rule classification used by search.
* DrawRulesInternals.h exposes only the cache access required by the
  historical tests; production search does not depend on it.

UCI and concurrency interfaces:

* UciProtocol.cpp interprets UCI and development commands.
* UciSearchController owns the search worker and controls its lifecycle.
* The controller gives each search its own copies of the root position and
  repetition history.
* Stop and quit are propagated cooperatively through the search.
* Only the last fully completed iteration may become the committed result.
* An incomplete iteration cannot replace an already committed score, PV, or
  bestmove.
* A legal fallback move remains available if interruption occurs before the
  first iteration is completed.
* UciReporter serializes all UCI output through a mutex.
* Exactly one bestmove is emitted for every completed or externally stopped
  go.
* The worker is joined after every tested termination path.

As a qualitative complement to validation, four games were played against

diagnostic material, not a statistical measurement of playing strength.

Conditions:

- normal starting position;
- Release|x64 executable;
- no opening book;
- no Syzygy tablebases;
- ponder disabled;

- one game with each color against each opponent.

Results:

- PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-002 vs. Zoidberg 0.1: 0-1;
- Zoidberg 0.1 vs. PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-002: 1-0;
- PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-002 vs. Shallow Blue 2.0.0: 1/2-1/2;

Cumulative result:

Game observations:

1. PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-002 vs. Zoidberg 0.1

From the opening, PHILOCHESS obtained a fully playable position, without
structural defects or serious coordination problems. The turning point came
with 17.exd5, an exchange that allowed Zoidberg to transform the center into a
lasting source of initiative, gain space, and restrict White's pieces. With
18.g4, PHILOCHESS addressed the kingside when the true urgency was to prevent
the double attack on d4. From that moment onward, Zoidberg coordinated its
forces more effectively and converted its central superiority into direct
pressure against the king. PHILOCHESS recognised the danger only after it had
materialized, revealing difficulty in anticipating forcing threats. In the
subsequent phase, it sought local solutions and material gains, while its
opponent exploited piece activity, coordination, and back-rank vulnerability
more effectively.

2. Zoidberg 0.1 vs. PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-002

With 9...Qd4, PHILOCHESS accepted sacrificing the exchange in return for
accelerating its development and strengthening its control of the center and
queenside. The compensation was sufficient to sustain a dynamic position,
although 17...Ng4 proved inaccurate. Castling would have allowed better
consolidation of piece coordination. On move 19, a tactical sequence also
appeared that offered good recovery chances, but PHILOCHESS did not detect it,
probably because it required greater calculation depth. After 22.Bxb4 and
23.Qxa7, Zoidberg won the a7-pawn, but Black used those tempi to stabilize its
position and achieve a clear advantage through its dangerous passed pawn. The
advantage disappeared with 26...Bh3, which allowed that pawn —until then the
main dynamic element of Black's position— to be eliminated. From that point,
the game returned to equilibrium, although Zoidberg gradually demonstrated a
more refined understanding of the pre-endgame, improved the coordination of
its pieces, and created new difficulties on the queenside.

3. PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-002 vs. Shallow Blue 2.0.0

Compared with its predecessor, PHILOCHESS handled the kingside better and,
with 14.g5, correctly closed that sector, avoiding the exchanges on h5 that
had previously made it easier for the opposing rook and knight to join the
attack. The errors 15...c5 and 16...c4 allowed White to reorganize its
pieces, gain space, and achieve a clear positional advantage. Conversion,
however, was once again uneven, and PHILOCHESS allowed part of its advantage
to slip away before regaining control as a result of further inaccuracies by
Shallow Blue. In the endgame, White's position was objectively winning, but
61.Kd2 moved the king away from the dangerous h4-pawn. Shallow Blue then
sacrificed its rook on g4 to clear that pawn's path, immediately transforming
a lost position into a winning one. PHILOCHESS reacted with great practical
precision and resorted to a repetition manoeuvre. Shallow Blue failed to
realize that it could still win and allowed threefold repetition to occur,
enabling PHILOCHESS to save half a point after going, within only a few moves,
from winning to losing.

4. Shallow Blue 2.0.0 vs. PHILOCHESS 0.3-alpha-002

Against Shallow Blue, PHILOCHESS reached the same pre-endgame that its
predecessor had played against this opponent, but this time handled it with
greater activity and better coordination. Instead of confining itself to
passive defence, it activated its pieces and simplified into a rook ending in
which it maintained the balance. The gradual reduction of material led to a
technically delicate ending, with a White passed pawn as the main source of
danger. In the final stages, PHILOCHESS had to find several practically unique
moves to preserve the draw and prevent promotion. Its king approached at the
right moment, cooperated with the rook, and controlled the critical squares.
Syzygy confirmed that this phase was mathematically drawn, although PHILOCHESS
played the entire game without tablebases. After the rooks were exchanged, the
Black king captured the last pawn. The game ended with only the two kings
remaining on the board, thus completing a technically correct defence that
was notably more active than that of its previous version.
